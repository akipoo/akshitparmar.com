Pilot 03 · Common Grounds

Somewhere people choose to stay.

Common Grounds is my side business: coffee, pickleball and padel on a covered rooftop, open from six in the morning to two at night. It is also the most successful thing I run.

Status OpenHours 6 am – 2 amResults in my letter

The bet

Build for what people choose to do.

HypothesisMake the optional things easy and people stay. The social life follows on its own.

Most commercial spaces in our cities are built for the things people have to do: buy, eat, leave. Nobody stays a minute longer than they need to.

Common Grounds is built the other way round. The coffee gets people in. The courts give them a reason to stay. And once people stay, they start talking to each other.

Life between buildings

Three kinds of reasons to be somewhere.

Jan Gehl, the Danish architect, sorted what people do in public space into three kinds. The necessary things happen anywhere, however bad the place is. The optional things only happen when the place is good enough to invite them. The social things happen as a result of the other two.

You can't build a friendship. You can build a place where one is likely.

01 · Necessary

What people come for anyway.

Coffee, food and clean washrooms, from six in the morning to two at night. People would come for these even if the place were ordinary.

02 · Optional

What they choose to stay for.

A game of pickleball or padel on a covered court, so the monsoon never cancels it. Nobody has to play. They choose to.

03 · Social

What happens because they stayed.

Families, friends and neighbours on the same court, tournaments and pop-ups with local brands. The one part I can't build directly. It follows.

The three kinds of activity are from Jan Gehl's book Life Between Buildings.
